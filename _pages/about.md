Welcome! I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Division of Geological and Planetary Sciences at Caltech, working with <a href='https://www.gps.caltech.edu/people/jean-philippe-avouac'>Prof. Jean-Philippe Avouac</a>. Before coming here, I obtained my PhD in Mechanics from the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL), where I was advised by <a href='https://www.epfl.ch/labs/gel/'>Prof. Brice Lecampion</a>.

My research combines theory, computation, and experiments to understand the physics of earthquakes and faults. During my PhD, I focused on fluid-driven frictional ruptures and their applications to injection-induced seismicity. While I continue to work and collaborate on these topics, my current research focuses largely on other interesting problems in earthquake science. Specifically, I am investigating slow earthquakes in subduction zones and their possible connection to the release of metamorphic fluids from dehydration reactions, fault segmentation patterns observed in historical and paleoseismic records along major plate boundaries, and the factors that control earthquake nucleation timing. Through collaborations, I also investigate the effects of fault geometric complexities on synchronization and alternation of earthquake timing patterns observed between neighboring faults, and possible mechanisms enabling the potential presence of water in the middle crust of Mars.

More broadly, I seek to understand how faults respond to both tectonic and anthropogenic loading, including subsurface fluid injections which are relevant to geo-energy technologies that are key to decarbonize our energy systems such a deep geothermal energy and CO2 geological storage. I am particularly interested in simple but fundamental questions: How do earthquakes start? How do they stop? What determines whether a fault slips slowly or rapidly? My research uses tools of theoretical, computational and experimental mechanics, to develop physics-based models that are informed by and explain geological and geophysical observations. Through this approach, I aim to advance our understanding of the physics and hazard posed by earthquakes, and support the development of subsurface technologies for decarbonization that are often constrained by induced seismicity.
